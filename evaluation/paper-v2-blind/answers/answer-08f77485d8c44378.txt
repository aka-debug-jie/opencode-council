**Decision**

The papers are conditional, not contradictory. The question actually unresolved for this service is not "is coordination necessary?" but: *which cross-partition invariants are non-I-confluent, and must coordination be paid at single-site serialization points on hot records, or globally across the whole transaction (2PC/Paxos or a global input order)?* The team cannot answer it because it lacks the invariant registry every mechanism presupposes.

**Evidence and assumptions**

Paper-3 (published): coordination-free execution is correct iff operations are I-confluent w.r.t. declared invariants (Theorem 1, §4.2, p.189), and "if invariants are incorrectly or incompletely specified… users should opt for… serializable transactions" (§4.3, p.190). Its result presupposes complete invariant knowledge — exactly what this service lacks. Table 2 (p.190) is decisive for inventory/accounts: a "greater-than" threshold under decrement is non-I-confluent (Claim 13) — i.e., "balance/inventory ≥ 0" under withdrawal *does* need coordination; specific-value uniqueness is non-confluent (Claim 3), while "some value" namespaced IDs are confluent (Claim 4). Recency is explicitly impossible without coordination: "if application reads face these requirements, coordination is required" (§5.3, p.191). Paper-1 never avoids coordination: external consistency costs TrueTime + 2PC + commit-wait ≥2ε (§4.1.2, p.7; ~5 ms, §5.1, p.9). Paper-2 removes 2PC but relocates coordination to a global input sequence and demands advance read/write sets (§3.1–3.2); dependent transactions are unsupported natively (§3.2.1).

Hidden assumption (inference): "coordination" has one meaning, and all three papers share the team's epistemic state. They do not: Spanner/Calvin assume no usable invariant knowledge and pay a global ordering; Paper-3 assumes full knowledge and pays only at non-confluent points (even one spinlocked record, 0.19% of cycles, §6.2, p.193). Different premises, so no genuine contradiction.

**Alternatives**

(1) *Scoped coordination* (Paper-3 §6.1 pattern): enumerate invariants; serialize only non-confluent decrement/ID points at their home partition; run the rest coordination-free. Works because the hard invariants are per-record thresholds; falsified by an unenumerated global invariant (cross-account sum, double-spend) or fresh-read requirements. Cheapest check: the shadow replay below.

(2) *Global coordination* (Paper-1 style): external consistency on all transfers. Works without invariant knowledge; falsified if commit-wait plus multi-site 2PC latency (F1: 103 ms multi-site commit, §5.4, p.11) breaks the latency budget. Cheapest check: toggle commit-wait on a staging path (§5.1 "1D" vs 1-replica).

(3) *Global sequencing* (Paper-2 style): deterministic order, no 2PC; falsified if transactions can't declare read/write sets (dependent balance checks); cheapest check: count dependent transactions in logs (§3.2.1).

**Failure modes**

Scoped: silent oversell/double-spend from an undeclared invariant (§4.3). Global: hot-record contention, partition blocking, over-coordination. Sequencing: OLLP reconnoitering restarts under contention.

**Falsification tests** (new proposal)

One-week trace audit (cheapest): classify each cross-partition transaction's (invariant, operation) pairs against Paper-3 Table 2 and mark recency reads. If the hot path is dominated by non-confluent decrements or fresh reads, scoping is unsupported. Then shadow-run a coordination-free merge with per-invariant validators and inject a partition: violations only at predicted non-confluent points confirm scoped coordination; violations elsewhere falsify it and reveal hidden invariants. This discriminates because the mechanisms predict different violation locations and overheads, and it costs only log replay, no production change.

**Open questions**

Which invariants are global vs regional? Must balance/inventory reads be fresh? Are sequential IDs required, or do namespaced IDs suffice (Paper-3 Claim 4; Paper-1 §5.1)? Who maintains the invariant registry as requirements evolve (§5.3)?
